Read this before the numbers

This tool was built in a few days, on a stack of stated assumptions. I didn't have the time — or the inside knowledge of what EveryoneSocial's customers actually optimize for — to be certain these are the metrics that matter most. The four categories and their weights are editorial judgment, not measurement: nobody has proven that these things cause employees to post, and we don't claim otherwise.

What isn't measured, on purpose: your employees' actual posting. LinkedIn doesn't permit that access, so a free tool claiming to measure it is either paying an enrichment vendor or estimating. This report only reads what your company has published on its own public site — which is also the part you can fix.

This report was pre-generated from evidence collected in late August 2026 rather than live — the honest reason is speed: a full live run takes up to a minute on large sites and I chose accuracy over optimizing that this week. Every observation below is dated and cited.

What you can hold us to: every number cites its public evidence, nothing unseen is guessed at, robots.txt is respected on every fetch, and when we couldn't read a site we say "we couldn't read you" instead of issuing a grade.

Issue

0 of 24 sampled pages name a human author

None of the 24 sampled content pages carry a named human author — no byline, no author schema, no rel=author. Content nobody signs is content nobody feels ownership of; advocacy runs on employees being visible in the work.

Evidence: content page sample — 0 of 24 sampled pages.

The fix: Add author bylines to content pages — a name, ideally with a link or an author block in the Article schema. Start with new posts; backfilling can wait.

Opportunity

No culture or life-at pages anywhere in your sitemap

Of 155 canonical URLs, zero classify as culture content — no life-at pages, employee stories, or team spotlights. This is the most common gap we see, and the most directly advocacy-shaped one: it is the content employees share proudest and first.

Evidence: sitemap URL classification.

The fix: Start small: one employee-story post a month, indexed in the sitemap. The section can grow from there.

Opportunity

Your shareable content lives in only 2 sections

All shareable content sits in: news_press, about. Variety matters for advocacy — different employees share different things (news, case studies, culture stories, events).

Evidence: sitemap URL classification.

The fix: Add at least one more content type employees would plausibly share — customer stories and culture posts are the usual gaps.

Evidence: robots.txt fetch.

The fix: Serve a plain-text robots.txt at the domain root. Even an empty or allow-all file makes your crawl policy explicit instead of undefined.

Not visible to us

Content freshness is not observable here

Your sitemap publishes lastmod dates, but they look machine-generated rather than edit history (62.6% of all lastmod values fall on a single day (2024-12-05), which is the signature of a site-wide rebuild rather than individual edits.), so we cannot read them as freshness and have not guessed.

Evidence: sitemap lastmod analysis.

The fix: Emit real last-modified dates in the sitemap. Credible freshness data helps every machine reader — search, AI, and tools like this one — represent you accurately.
