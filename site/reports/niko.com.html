Read this before the numbers

This tool was built in a few days, on a stack of stated assumptions. I didn't have the time — or the inside knowledge of what EveryoneSocial's customers actually optimize for — to be certain these are the metrics that matter most. The four categories and their weights are editorial judgment, not measurement: nobody has proven that these things cause employees to post, and we don't claim otherwise.

What isn't measured, on purpose: your employees' actual posting. LinkedIn doesn't permit that access, so a free tool claiming to measure it is either paying an enrichment vendor or estimating. This report only reads what your company has published on its own public site — which is also the part you can fix.

This report was pre-generated from evidence collected in late August 2026 rather than live — the honest reason is speed: a full live run takes up to a minute on large sites and I chose accuracy over optimizing that this week. Every observation below is dated and cited.

What you can hold us to: every number cites its public evidence, nothing unseen is guessed at, robots.txt is respected on every fetch, and when we couldn't read a site we say "we couldn't read you" instead of issuing a grade.

What we found — most fixable first

Ranked by impact × ease of fixing, not by category. Counts describe the pages we actually read, and each finding cites them.

Issue

Your homepage share card is missing og:image

The homepage lacks og:image. When anyone — including your own employees — shares your homepage link, the preview card renders incomplete (no image means most platforms show a bare grey box).

Evidence: homepage HTML.

The fix: Add the missing tag to the homepage template: og:image. Minutes of work, visible on every share afterward.

Issue

We could not retrieve a careers page

We tried 2 careers location(s) (https://www.niko.com/careers, https://www.niko.com/jobs) and none returned a readable page. Candidates researching you through tools and AI assistants hit the same wall.

Evidence — careers page probes
  • https://www.niko.com/careers — HTTP 404
  • https://www.niko.com/jobs — HTTP 404

The fix: Serve a crawlable careers page at /careers and link it from the homepage.

Opportunity

No structured data on your homepage

The homepage carries no JSON-LD schema.org markup. Machine readers — search engines and AI assistants alike — get no structured statement of who you are (Organization, name, logo, sameAs profiles).

Evidence: homepage HTML.

The fix: Add an Organization JSON-LD block to the homepage: name, url, logo, and sameAs links to your official social profiles.

Opportunity

No culture or life-at pages anywhere in your sitemap

Of 378 canonical URLs, zero classify as culture content — no life-at pages, employee stories, or team spotlights. This is the most common gap we see, and the most directly advocacy-shaped one: it is the content employees share proudest and first.

Evidence: sitemap URL classification.

The fix: Start small: one employee-story post a month, indexed in the sitemap. The section can grow from there.

Issue

Only 0 shareable pages on the whole site

After collapsing localized duplicates and filtering non-content pages, 0 pages of shareable content remain (of 378 canonical URLs). An advocacy program needs a supply of things worth sharing; below roughly 30 pages there is not enough material to run one, regardless of quality.

Evidence: sitemap URL classification.

The fix: Before any tooling, build the supply: a blog or insights section, case studies, or press — content a person would attach their name to.

Not visible to us

Content freshness is not observable here

Your sitemap publishes lastmod dates, but they look machine-generated rather than edit history (81.6% of all lastmod values fall on a single day (2026-08-29), which is the signature of a site-wide rebuild rather than individual edits.; The entire inventory's lastmod range spans only 184 days, which is implausibly short for 752 pages and suggests the dates were regenerated.), so we cannot read them as freshness and have not guessed.

Evidence: sitemap lastmod analysis.

The fix: Emit real last-modified dates in the sitemap. Credible freshness data helps every machine reader — search, AI, and tools like this one — represent you accurately.
